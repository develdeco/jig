Both verdicts begin with the one step, never by hand and never `jig ticket new` for the work: `jig chart new <name> --title "<t>" --need "<the need>"` mints the issue (the title, the need as its body) and the chart that records it, prints the issue id and the chart folder, and refuses an empty need or a bad name before minting. For a tracker item jig can bind, either verdict takes `jig chart new <name> --item <n>` instead (`--title` then names the chart only), and jig copies the item's title and text into the issue. `--need`, `--issue <ID>` and `--item` exclude one another; `--issue` charts an issue that exists. If the chart write fails, the issue stands: repair with `jig chart new <name> --title "<t>" --issue <ID>`; `jig validate <issue>` lists `issue has no chart` until then. The map's Destination holds one line naming the issue; the need is not copied there.

A brief verdict: after the step write one entry in `charts/<name>/tickets.yaml` (a task: `title`, and a `body` scoped to the work) and run `jig graduate <name>`, which mints the task. Draft its `brief.md` and `slices.yaml` in the task's folder; the task is what the person approves and what `jig next` prompts. The chart validates. The name is the title's lowercase slug unless the person names one.

A chart verdict: after the step write the entries and graduate them; the issue exists from the verdict, not only after graduation. Decision tickets: one per grilling session, entered as they resolve, each with a `key:` (its store key; omit if the store declares one). `jig validate <name>` lists every problem and mints nothing; `jig graduate <name>` creates the entries without ids and writes them back, a re-run makes only new ones.

A batch spanning areas takes a chart verdict, ordering its tickets.

A chart verdict ends a ticket run: report what graduated and what `jig next <chart>` says, then read the prompt's intent from its words (examples, no phrase list); unsure, ask.

- **No intent** (nothing past the brief: "fix this: ...", "fix this, go with your recommendations"): name the prompts that launch one ticket ("Take <ticket> to PR"), several ("Take A and B to PR") or the chart ("Take <chart> to PR"), stop, ask nothing.
- **Intent, not fully explicit** ("take it to PR", "fix this and open a PR", "take it from brief to PR, go with your recommendations"): ask once to confirm the launch it implied (the chart, or the tickets carrying a named ticket's work), "not now" the other answer; the run goes with your recommendations only if the yes says so; unattended, stop. Why: a ticket's terms ask for a ticket run, which a chart verdict ends.
- **Fully explicit** (recommendations and working to the end, whatever the shape: "go with your recommendations, keep going until it is all done", "fix it end to end with your recommendations, I'll be away"): say why in one line, run `jig skill chart`, ask nothing.

A ticket that already exists and that no chart lists (a backlog ticket) takes either verdict the same way: the one step still makes the issue and its chart, and the chart's entry carries that ticket's `id` with its `title` and a `body` scoped to its part, so graduate keeps it `existing` and mints no second task. A ticket a chart already lists (a graduated task) takes no step and makes nothing: it is drafted in its own folder.

A chart holds every ticket its need takes. When `jig recall` names an open ticket of another chart near the work, or a fog item is about another chart's tickets, and this chart's model makes that ticket's goal native, fold it in. Judge that by meaning, never by matching words. Fold it in two steps. The moved tickets enter this chart's entries with their ids as the entries are written. The closes and edge moves follow `jig graduate`, naming the task ids it prints, because `jig close --by` takes only a ticket that exists:

- Once graduated, close each ticket this chart supersedes with `jig close <T> --reason superseded --by <task> --note "<why>"`, naming the task that replaces it.
- Move the rest, with their ids, into this chart's entries (before `jig graduate`) and out of the other chart's `tickets.yaml`. A ticket two charts list is a shared task, not a fold. A multi-task moves with its tasks, never a task alone, or the task's pull request still goes into the other chart's multi-task. Taking an entry out shifts the positional `#k` refs after it: rewrite each ref the move shifts, in both charts and in the moved entry's own refs (a ref to a ticket that has an id can be written as its id).
- Mark each moved ticket that has a brief for a rebrief with a note atop its `brief.md` saying what changed. Editing it withdraws the approval, so `jig next` puts it back at its brief.
- Once graduated, move an edge to a closed ticket onto its replacement with `jig depend <T> --on <P>` (keep `--kind stacked` when the old edge was stacked), and clear the old edge with `jig depend <T> --on <closed> --clear` (without `--on`, `--clear` removes every edge of the ticket). A superseded blocker releases the tickets that wait for it.

Never leave such a ticket as an edge out of the chart. A ticket that serves another need stays where it is, and an edge to it is fine. Both maps record the fold: this chart's as a decision, the other's as a note at its top. An earlier chart left with no open ticket has its issue closed as superseded by this chart's issue, only when none of its tickets published or landed; otherwise that issue keeps what it holds and lands it.
