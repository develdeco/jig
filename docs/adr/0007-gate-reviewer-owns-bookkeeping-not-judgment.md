# Gate reviewer: jig owns bookkeeping, never judgment

The gate reviewer traces to three no-mistakes lessons: incremental rounds, mechanical bundling of findings, and triage at the human seam. An earlier branch (PR #8) kept that vocabulary but replaced the mechanism with rules in five places: a hand-made `class` list (typo, dead code, behavior, security, ...) stood in for the model's own judgment of who acts on a finding; the reviewer had to write one closure per open finding, with a miscount failing the round; a clean result carried no evidence of what was actually read; a repeat of a dismissed finding was matched by normalizing and comparing its title text; and the eval corpus's gold was hand-written cases matched by a per-case title regex, widened case by case as reviews drifted from it. Each of these decides something - who should act, whether a problem recurred, whether two findings are the same - by pattern-matching the model's prose or by a jig-side rule standing in for a judgment the model or the human should make.

This rework replaces every one of them without adding a new special case: `action` (fix, ask, note) is the reviewer's own judgment, checked only for being one of three known values; identity across rounds is carried only through the reviewer's own `prior` field, never inferred from title text; a result must name every file it read (`reviewed_paths`), and an open finding only clears when its file was actually covered; a dismissed finding stays dismissed forever once `prior` names it, with no auto-dismiss heuristic beside it. What stayed unchanged from PR #8: the disk contract itself (`review.json` in, `result.json` out), strict structural parsing, the read-only guard, the gate-lease restore points, `reviewed_sha` scope anchoring at the merge base, and fix slices as the only way a finding turns into work - review has no back edges.

Two more places were cut rather than replaced: `rung: cheapest`, which pinned mechanical fixes to the cheapest model and overrode a staircase that already starts there, and the reviewer-eval title-pattern matcher, whose gold cases and phrasing tables grew every time a real review's wording drifted from the pattern. Recording which files a reviewer session actually opened (rather than trusting its own `reviewed_paths` claim) and proving a fix red-then-green with a finding-specific oracle were both considered and left for later: the former needs headless running for real, and the latter is covered today only by the next round's review of the same file.
